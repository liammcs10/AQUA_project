Your exact definition gives an uninformative ROC. If TP means within the tolerance and FP means outside it, in the same trials, then FPR = 1 − TPR at every tolerance. Every curve is then the diagonal and the AUC is always 0.5. So, as we agreed, the notebook has both of these:

Hit rate vs tolerance. This is P(|error| ≤ tol), plotted for each condition, with the normalised area under it as the AUC.
Set TOL_MODE = 'isi' or 'ms' at the top of the cell.
In 'isi' mode the tolerance is a multiple (0–20) of each condition's mean ISI. The mean ISI comes from CPA_cusum_N100_spikes.pickle, pooled over trials, ignoring the first 200 ms.
In 'ms' mode the tolerance runs from 0 to 1000 ms.
ROC against no-change trials. The tolerance is swept as you suggested, but the false positives come from the step-0 trials of the same neuron and noise level:
TPR(tol) = P(|error| ≤ tol | step h)
FPR(tol) = P(|error| ≤ tol | step 0), i.e. how often an estimate lands that close to 5000 ms with no change at all.
The AUC is the probability that the error with a step is smaller than the error without one. I computed it exactly, and it matches the area under the plotted curve.
The ROC curve and its AUC don't depend on the tolerance units. Every point on the curve uses the same tolerance for both sets of trials, so measuring it in ms or in ISIs traces the same curve. The ISI option therefore changes only the hit-rate plots and the dots on the ROC, which mark tol = 1 mean ISI (or tol_ref_ms in 'ms' mode).
