
Key points:
  - We use the FLUSS algorithm to identify the change point. This is a lightweight, model-free estimate of the change point. 
  It also comes with a CAC value which can be interpreted as a 'confidence' or 'amount of evidence'

  - Important metrics: estimated change point time, CAC (or 'confidence')


The autapse may change output firing patterns in such a way that the FLUSS algorithm can identify
the change point from membrane potential data directly.

  CPA_data_N10.PICKLE
    - 10 repeats for noise values. Noise is sampled at 10,000 Hz!
    - There is a critical value of the autapse parameter where the change point detection is optimal
    and performs better than other autapse parameter values. This is due to the step offering a transition from tonic -> bursting - PURELY COINCIDENCE?
    - Area at this value reaches it maximum, equivalent to higher autapse strengths despite the decrease in activity corresponding to

    - This critical value aligns with the 'bursting' regime and is slightly above the experimentally recorded value. 
  
  CPA_data_N100.PICKLE
    - 100 repeats for each noise value. Noise is now sampled at 50Hz!
    - No longer the bump in performance at moderate autapse strengths. 
    - Performance (AUC) increases up to moderate f, then decreases between 
    f = {200-300} and increases again up to f = 350, then decreases...
    - Performance decreases at 400 due to the neuron being a new regime. This is due to the neuron being in a CHAOTIC regime
    before the step and transitioning to tonic firing after - PURELY COINCIDENCE.
    - Some neurons see a drop in performance despite a larger step height - This is due to long high-frequency 
    transients after the step, the changepoint is then assigned to the end of this transient.

DIVERGENCE MEASURES (DECODABILITY): (data = CPA_cusum_N100_llr_pair_div.pickle)
 - every measure falls as autapse strength goes up starting at about f = 200-250
 - JS divergence saturates by dH = 10pA for f <= 200. For f = 350, it hasn't saturated yet by dH = 50pA.
      -> JS is the better metric over Jeffrey's as the latter is uncapped when distributions don't overlap at all.
*** autapse reduces the amount of information about the stimulus and an optimal decoder would perform worse ***
      -> due to broader distributions. Spike trains are noisier due to the autapse.
      -> Using model-free comparisons of distributions shows the same behaviour: the distance between pre- and post- degrades
      as the autapse is made stronger suggesting the signal is less decodable with the autapse present.
 - There is a regime where the jeffrey's divergence is high but the CUSUM algorithm is missing information - this is due 
 to the log-likelihood being clipped at +- 10
      -> Change point estimate is unaffected with and without the clip. Accuracy is limited by spike timing. 
 - at large h, score and score per s understate how well the non-autaptic neuron does. 
 - Fisher information drops 10x from f = 0 to f = 400.
